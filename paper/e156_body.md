In Africa-site HIV randomized trials registered on ClinicalTrials.gov, how often do studies meet a practical shortlist for cheaper and faster delivery? Eighty completed, results-posted HIV trials formed the Africa benchmark, and eighteen met pre-specified transfer shortlist criteria on enrollment, sites, countries, and prospective registration. We performed a registry-based descriptive scan, compared Africa-site records with a global benchmark, and treated the shortlist proportion as the primary estimand with Wilson intervals. The shortlist proportion was 0.23, with 95% confidence interval 0.15 to 0.33. Median results-reporting lag was 566.5 days, interquartile range 418.5 to 1061.0, while South Africa, Kenya, and Uganda dominated the Africa-site footprint. These findings suggest HIV has reusable lean designs, but many appear to depend on stronger existing trial infrastructure and concentrated regional hubs under mature laboratory, adherence, and follow-up systems already in place. Interpretation is limited by registry proxies rather than true cost data, sparse result-linked publications, and no direct measure of African intellectual leadership.
